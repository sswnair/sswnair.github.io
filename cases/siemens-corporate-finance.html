Siemens Corporate Finance and Capital Allocation

The group tested how Siemens' operating outlook could translate into free cash flow, distributions and strategic investment capacity.

Academic group projectFY2025 reported base; Q3 FY2026 results published August 6, 2026

Operating question

Can Siemens fund strategic investment and shareholder returns while protecting cash generation?

€84.42bn FY2026E revenue proxy Base case
€10.80bn FY2026E free cash flow Base case
€6.48bn Retained free cash flow After 40% distribution
€5.98bn Severe downside FCF 0% growth, 12% margin
The base case retains €6.48bn after a 40% distribution

Scenario result

At 7% comparable growth, a 15.5% Industrial Business margin and 0.85x cash conversion, the base case produces a €84.42bn revenue proxy and €10.80bn of free cash flow. A 40% distribution leaves €6.48bn of retained free cash flow.

The decision rule is explicit: if comparable growth falls below 3% or Industrial Business margin falls below 13.5%, reduce discretionary distributions and pace acquisitions. The severe downside produces €5.98bn of free cash flow.

What could weaken cash capacity

  • Comparable growth is applied to FY2025 group revenue as a planning proxy, so currency and portfolio effects can create a gap to reported revenue.
  • Cash conversion, distribution rates and investment allocations are analytical cases rather than Siemens policy.
  • Integration costs and working-capital pressure can reduce cash available for acquisitions and shareholder returns.
