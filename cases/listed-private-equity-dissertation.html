Private Market Liquidity in Public View

Interest-rate shocks and NAV discounts in listed private equity vehicles, tested across a hand-built 25-fund panel.

MSc Finance dissertationSubmitted July 2026

Research question

Are UK interest-rate changes associated with wider listed private equity discounts, and do public prices lead later NAV marks?

Finding · Discounts carry information about later NAV marks

Research finding

In a fund fixed-effects panel, a one percentage point rise in the 10-year gilt yield is associated with a price-to-NAV discount 6.1 percentage points wider. The estimate is significant under Driscoll-Kraay inference (p = 0.033) and marginal under fund-and-quarter clustering (p = 0.076), so it is treated as associational rather than causal.

The central result is that current discounts forecast next-quarter NAV returns. The deepest-discount quintile averages −1.0%, versus +4.2% for the narrowest, a 5.2 percentage point endpoint spread. The predicted market-frictions amplification is not supported.

What could change the answer

Risks and limitations

  • The design estimates associations rather than a causal effect.
  • The panel is unbalanced and contains 25 London-listed vehicles, so inference is sensitive to the treatment of common quarterly shocks.
  • NAV dates may differ from publication dates, and NAV age captures only mechanical staleness.
  • Liquidity and dividend yield may be endogenous; the credit-spread control is a euro high-yield proxy; and the OIS series starts later than the main panel.
  • Listed private equity does not map directly onto unlisted private equity.
  • Morningstar and Refinitiv extracts are licensed and are not redistributed.
