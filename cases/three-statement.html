Microsoft Three-Statement Model

I linked Microsoft segment assumptions through the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement to test how growth and capital spending affect cash generation.

Self-directed public-data case studyHistorical FY2025 reported base with an archived forecast through FY2030E

Operating question

How do segment growth, margins, working capital and capital expenditure flow through the three statements?

$428.36bn FY2030E revenue FY2025-vintage forecast
$158.03bn FY2030E free cash flow FY2025-vintage forecast
$36.41bn FY2030E ending cash FY2025-vintage forecast
$8.79bn FY2030E ending debt FY2025-vintage forecast
The FY2025-vintage case reaches $158.03bn of 2030 free cash flow and ends with net cash

Forecast result

In this FY2025-vintage planning case, revenue grows from $281.72bn in FY2025A to $428.36bn in FY2030E. Free cash flow reaches $158.03bn after correcting the scenario drivers and cash-flow assumption references.

The case ends FY2030E with $36.41bn of cash and $8.79bn of debt. Balance-sheet, cash-flow, retained-earnings, debt and working-capital controls reconcile across every displayed period.

Forecast risks

  • Cloud demand and data-center capital spending may not follow the assumed path.
  • Other long-term liabilities are used as a residual forecast category in this simplified public-data model.
  • FY2026E is an archived analyst scenario, not current actual results.
  • Forecast assumptions are analytical estimates rather than Microsoft guidance.
