UCITS Limit Monitor

Checks a UCITS fund against its investment limits using public data. Issuer concentration, the 5/10/40 rule, deposits, OTC counterparties and combined exposure, then global exposure with VaR and a backtest of the VaR model itself.

Self-directed Python project153 testsSample data as of October 16, 2026

The question

Would this fund pass its UCITS limit checks this morning, and can its VaR model be trusted?

41.9% 5/10/40 total Cap 40%
20.5% One bank, combined Cap 20%
1.05x Relative VaR Limit 2.0x
7 VaR exceptions 250 days, yellow zone
The sample fund breaches three limits

What the sample run shows

No single issuer is above 10%, but six of them sit above 5% and add up to 41.9%, so as a standard UCITS the fund fails the 5/10/40 rule. The same holdings pass as an index tracker, which is the room Article 53 gives concentrated index funds. Northwind Markets, which is not a bank, breaches the 5% OTC counterparty limit on its own. Corrib Bank is inside each limit separately, with 18.5% of NAV on deposit and 2% as an OTC counterparty, but the two come to 20.5%, over the 20% cap for a single body.

The VaR model was beaten 7 times in 250 days against about 2.5 expected. That is the Basel yellow zone, and over the 4 that have to be reported. Six of the seven fall in one volatile stretch in the spring, three of them on consecutive days. A normal-distribution VaR does worse on the same returns, with 10 exceptions and a red zone, because the returns have fatter tails than a normal curve allows.

Work completed

What it checks

Diversification

  • Single issuer: 10% of NAV, or 20% and 35% for index trackers
  • The 5/10/40 rule
  • Deposits: 20% with any one bank
  • OTC counterparties: 10% for banks, 5% for anyone else
  • Combined exposure to one body: 20%
  • Share classes of one issuer counted together

Global exposure and backtesting

  • Historical VaR at 99%, 20 days, from 250 daily returns
  • Absolute VaR: 20% of NAV
  • Relative VaR: twice the reference portfolio
  • Rolling one-day forecasts with no look-ahead
  • Kupiec and Christoffersen tests
  • Basel traffic-light zones and the reporting trigger

How the code is organized

data.py
Downloads and parses an ETF's published holdings and daily prices, with a local cache
issuers.py
Turns security names into issuer keys and totals the weight per issuer
limits.py
The diversification checks
var.py
Historical and parametric VaR, scaling to 20 days, rolling forecasts
backtest.py
Kupiec, Christoffersen and conditional coverage tests, Basel zones
exposure.py
Absolute and relative VaR limits
engine.py
Runs the checks in order and collects the findings
report.py
The HTML report, findings CSV and breach log

Limits

What it leaves out

  • Holdings come from the fund's published file, which can lag the administrator's books by a day.
  • Cash, FX and futures lines don't count toward issuer limits, and derivatives are not looked through.
  • Companies in the same group are not yet treated as one body.
  • Most plain equity ETFs use the commitment approach, not VaR. The VaR checks run on them here to show the method.
  • National rules and a fund's prospectus can be stricter than the directive.
