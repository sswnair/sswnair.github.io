Euro Curve and Rate Risk

Rebuilds the euro area government yield curve from the six numbers the ECB publishes for it every business day, prices a bond portfolio off that curve, and measures its interest rate risk: DV01, key-rate DV01s and the six standard Basel rate shocks.

Self-directed Python project225 testsValuation date September 30, 2026

How much does this portfolio lose when rates move, and where on the curve does the risk sit?

What the sample run shows

Ten made-up euro government bonds, maturing between 2027 and 2056, priced off a made-up year of daily curves in the ECB's format. On September 30, 2026 the portfolio is worth €238.98m, yields 2.90% on average and has a DV01 of €203,163.

A 225bp fall in rates gains €15.36m more than the same rise loses. That's convexity, and the first-order estimate from the key-rate DV01s, which comes out the same size both ways, misses it. The steepener loses money even though it cuts short rates, because it only cuts them out to about three years, and over 90% of the portfolio's DV01 sits at five years and beyond.

Work completed

From the curve to the risk

The curve

  • Zero, forward and par rates from the Svensson parameters
  • A check against the ECB's published zero rates
  • A fresh fit to those rates on a grid of taus
  • Gaps in the history and one-day moves over 15bp

The bonds and the risk

  • Actual/actual (ICMA) accrued interest and yields
  • Macaulay and modified duration, convexity
  • DV01 for a parallel move in the zero curve
  • Key-rate DV01s at nine tenors
  • The six Basel shocks, every bond repriced in full
  • A key-rate estimate next to each result

Code

How the code is organized

ecb.py
Downloads the ECB's curve data and reads its CSV format
svensson.py
Zero and forward rates, discount factors and par yields; fitting the curve
curve.py
One day's curve, and shifted copies of it for bumps and shocks
bonds.py
Coupon dates, accrued interest, prices, yields, duration and convexity
risk.py
DV01 and key-rate DV01s
scenarios.py
The six rate shocks, full revaluation and the key-rate estimate
portfolio.py
The analysis for one valuation date
checks.py
The curve against the published rates, the refit, gaps and big moves

Limits

What it leaves out

  • Every bond is priced off one curve with no spread, so a bond that trades rich or cheap to the curve gets a model price.
  • Prices are for settlement on the curve date rather than two business days later.
  • Annual coupons only, with no odd first or last periods.
  • Shocked rates have no floor, and the shocks are instantaneous, with no carry or roll-down.
