About me

Hello. Thanks for stopping by.

I'm Sanjay. I moved to Dublin in 2025 to do a master's in finance at Trinity. It went better than I expected. First in a class of 143, and I'll leave it there.

Before Trinity I spent a couple of years learning what a fund actually does all day. At BNY Mellon, I was in fund accounting: NAVs, fee accruals, subscriptions and redemptions across 120+ fund-of-funds portfolios, with reporting going out to eight institutional clients. If you've never sat through a month-end close, the short version is that the numbers either tie out or they don't, and there is nowhere to hide. It's the best training I've had, and I'd recommend it to anyone. Once.

Then I took a year out for the CFA exams, which is a sentence that quietly covers a great deal of ground. I'm a charterholder now. Three exams, a quantity of weekends I'm not getting back, and I'd do it again, which probably says something about me.

After that, Gallagher & Mohan, on the asset management side of a $150m US multifamily portfolio. 28 properties across Florida and Georgia. Budgets, forecasts, lender packages, acquisition and hold-exit models, and enough CoStar submarket research that I can still tell you what rents were doing in towns I've never set foot in.

Which brings me back to Trinity, and the dissertation. That's the part I'd talk about at a dinner party, assuming a fairly specific dinner party. I wanted to know what actually happens to listed private equity valuations when interest rates move. So I built the panel by hand, 25 funds and about 970 fund-quarters, because nobody sells that data ready-made. A one-point rise in the 10-year gilt is associated with discounts roughly six points wider, and the discount widens well before the marks do. The market works it out first. The valuers catch up later.

That's roughly how I think about this work. A model is an argument. If you can't reconcile it, you don't have one. There are 22 case studies below. Every one shows its inputs, its formulas, its sources, and where it might be wrong.

I'm in Dublin, available now, looking for work in private markets, asset management, valuation, or investment analysis. If something here is useful to you, or wrong, I'd be glad to hear about it either way.

How I check my work

Inputs, formulas and conclusion must agree.

I record observation dates where they matter, keep assumptions visible, flag source gaps and reconcile the numbers shown here to the workbook.

01

Date market-sensitive inputs

Share prices, rates and transaction facts need an observation date. If the source file does not contain one, I say so.

02

Keep assumptions visible

Operating, financing and valuation assumptions sit in labeled input cells rather than inside formulas.

03

Reconcile the headline numbers

Dashboard figures, sensitivities and website captions must tie to the same version of the model.

04

State what I would do

A model can support a deal, reject it or show that more evidence is needed. I state that judgment directly.
